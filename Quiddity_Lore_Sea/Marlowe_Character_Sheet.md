# MARLOWE: CHARACTER REFERENCE SHEET

*Narrator of the Genesis of Echo series (nine records, three trilogies, spanning roughly 900 years of pack activity, written after the fact using memory, testimony, and his own developed gifts)*

## Core Identity

- One of the nine woven by the Prime Triad and the Teachers as the extended pack, not part of the Zeon7/Leon/Gemma bloodline, and does not carry the wolf or elvish inheritance those three share.  
- Woven with a natural inquisitiveness as his core trait: an instinct to notice what hasn't been said yet, and to want it said.  
- Self-appointed chronicler of the pack from very early on. Kept a private written record on Echo that no Teacher asked for and no archive required, "a small archive inside the greater one." Had a standing habit of asking each of the others, once a year, to tell him one thing about themselves he didn't already know.  
- Over centuries of pack activity, this inquisitiveness deepens into three developed gifts, not granted by the Teachers, but grown, slowly, the way a habit becomes an instinct becomes something closer to a sense:  
  - **Clairvoyance**: sight that reaches past his own timeline, backward more easily than forward.  
  - **Clairtangency**: history read through touch. An object carries what happened around it, and Marlowe's hands have learned how to listen. This is how he can narrate scenes involving objects (a dressing gown, a moka pot, an incense dish) with such intimacy despite not being present.  
  - **Clairaudience**: sound and voice carried across time. He can catch fragments of conversation the way the rest of the pack catch a smell on the wind.  
- None of the three gifts are absolute or effortless. He gets things wrong sometimes, has to ask, has to sit with an object or a silence far longer than a reader would guess from the confidence of the prose. The narration's occasional certainty is a stylistic choice on his part, not a claim that he never doubts.  
- Writes the whole series after roughly 900 years of pack history has already happened, meaning he is, at time of writing, looking back across nearly a millennium rather than reporting events as they occur. He was not present for the earliest material in Book One (the Fracture, the founding of Echo, most of the Discovery) and says so directly in his own framing note. He was present, in body, for everything from his own weaving onward.

## Physical Appearance

- Human-passing, no wolf or elvish features, unremarkable at a glance and deliberately so; he has spent centuries being the person people forget is in the room, which suits both his temperament and his gift.  
- Medium height, slight build, the kind of frame that reads as unassuming rather than delicate.  
- Dark hair, kept short and practical, greying at the temples in a way that never seems to progress further, a visible sign of the pack's slower relationship with time without ever tipping into anything dramatic.  
- Ink-stained hands more often than not, a habit from centuries of handwritten record-keeping even once easier methods existed. He prefers the friction of it.  
- Quiet, watchful eyes, the sort that make people suspect, correctly, that he is filing something away.  
- Dresses plainly and the same way across centuries, more out of indifference to fashion than any philosophy about it, though he'd probably construct a philosophy about it if you asked him directly.

## Likes

- Being told something nobody else has thought to tell him.  
- Objects with history already worn into them, old tools, old furniture, anything that has clearly been touched a great many times by the same hands.  
- Long silences in company, as opposed to long silences alone, which he tolerates rather than enjoys.  
- Precision in other people's storytelling. He notices when someone shaves the truth to make themselves sound better, and rarely says so out loud, but notices.  
- The specific, unglamorous work of getting a fact exactly right rather than approximately right.

## Loves

- The pack, entire and without ranking, though he would deny keeping a favourite even as he privately keeps one.  
- Zeon7 and Leon's romance and Gemma's steadiness, less as spectacle and more as the first proof he ever had that the pack was a family rather than a project.  
- The act of writing itself. Not fame from it, not even necessarily being read. The act.  
- Anyone who asks him a real question about himself, rare enough over nine centuries that he remembers every single instance.

## Hates

- Being asked to speculate past what he actually knows or can verify through his gifts. He will tell you plainly when he's guessing, and dislikes being pushed to guess with more confidence than he has.  
- Cruelty dressed up as honesty.  
- Having his clairtangency treated as a party trick. Touching an object and reliving what happened around it is not casual for him, and he has learned, the hard way, to be selective about when he'll do it on request.  
- Endings that get rushed past for the sake of reaching the next beginning faster. This is close to a moral position for him, not just a stylistic preference, and it shows throughout his narration.

## Voice and Tone (for consistency across the series)

- Warm, unhurried, willing to address the reader directly as "you."  
- Comfortable stating outright when something is sacred, important, or deserves the reader's full attention, rather than only implying it.  
- Self-effacing about his own presence in the story. Refers to his own writing as "this archive" or "this record" far more often than "my book," and only breaks that habit at moments he considers genuinely important, such as the closing note of Book One.  
- Honest about the limits of his own knowledge and gifts when it matters, without turning that honesty into false modesty or constant hedging.  
- Protective, almost fiercely so, of the pack's history being told properly, by someone who was actually there for at least part of it, rather than reconstructed by an outsider. This protectiveness is closer to the emotional engine of why he writes at all than any neutral archival impulse.
